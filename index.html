TRAINING MANUAL Core concepts & frameworks no exam tips · pure facts
01 Inbound sales fundamentals
Inbound strategy
Identify people who might already be interested in your offering. Help the buyer make progress in defining or solving their problem.
Process vs. strategy
A sales process describes the steps a seller takes; an inbound sales strategy describes the steps a buyer takes.
Lead response time
Contact inbound leads preferably within minutes of receiving them.
Message length
Voicemails under 15s; emails under 200 words. Keep it short.
Outreach focus
Reference the buyer and their situation more than the seller.
02 Buyer types & personas
Active buyer
Actively researching a goal or challenge. Most often in the Consideration stage.
Passive buyer
A good fit for your offering but not looking to buy right now. Do not wait to identify active buyers before identifying passive ones.
Inbound lead
A person who visited your website and identified themselves in some way.
Trigger event
Anything indicating you could provide immediate value (press releases, job postings, social mentions of keywords or competitors).
ICP vs. persona
An ICP broadly describes a target market (company type or population segment); buyer personas define specific sorts of people within that market.
Note: Submitting a form on your website is an inbound lead, not a trigger event.
03 Buyer's journey & salesperson's role
Awareness
Buyer is identifying a challenge or opportunity. Role: help them define goals and challenges.
Consideration
Buyer evaluates different approaches to solve a challenge. Role: help them understand the different ways they might address a goal or challenge.
Decision
Buyer is choosing a specific solution. Role: help weigh pros and cons of your solution relative to other options.
04 The 4 phases of inbound sales strategy
Identify

Find good-fit leads from a large pool. Research active/passive buyers. Do not help leads identify goals here — that's Explore.

Connect

Outreach focus. Transition to Explore only when the buyer confirms interest in discussing a goal or challenge.

Explore

Act as expert, help sort through goals/challenges. Guide to right conclusion, even if it's not you. Diagnose first — no feature pitching.

Advise

Present a plan closing the gap between where they are now and where they want to be. No generic company info.

05 The CGP, TCI, BA framework
CGP Current situation
  • Goals What they want to achieve.
  • Challenges What is stopping them.
  • Plans How they intend to fix it. "Thinking about moving into a larger facility."
TCI Urgency & impact
  • Timeline When they need it done. "Before end of quarter."
  • Consequences What happens if they fail. "I'll lay off employees."
  • Implications What happens if they succeed. "Open a second location."
BA Logistics
  • Budget Ask openly how much they plan to invest.
  • Authority Ask who else needs to be involved. Avoid "Are you the right person?"

Agenda analysis · spotting flaws

Covers CGP + TCI but skips budget / sign-off → needs BA improvement.
Skips the prospect's specific goals → needs CGP improvement.
Skips what happens if they fail / succeed → needs TCI improvement.
06 Presentations & closing
  1. Main goal — provide value to the prospect.
  2. How to begin — recap previous conversations to ensure a shared understanding.
  3. Include — confirm timeline, discuss purchasing process, advise on best path to goals.
  4. Avoid — showing as many features as possible; case studies unless specifically asked for or highly relevant.
  5. 1–10 closing technique — help the buyer weigh pros and cons. If they give a number lower than 6, back up to Explore and figure out what you missed. Do not pick a number for them or offer an immediate discount.
  6. Junior employees — treat them the same as your target persona; they're likely researching for the decision-maker.
  7. Determine timeline — ask when they need to achieve the goal, then work backwards to set the contract-sign date.
